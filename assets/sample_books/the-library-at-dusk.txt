The Library at Dusk
A sample story for The Reading Room

Chapter 1

The library did not close at dusk. That was the first thing Amara learned, and the last thing she ever forgot.

She had come for a single book — a thin volume on the history of paper — and stayed because the light through the tall windows turned the dust into gold. The shelves rose higher than she could reach, and the ladder on its brass rail waited patiently, as if it knew she would need it.

"Do you read here often?" asked the old librarian, without looking up from his desk.

"Not often enough," Amara said.

He smiled. "Then you have come to the right place. This library keeps its own hours. It opens when you need it, and it closes only when you are ready to leave."

Chapter 2

Amara chose a chair by the window, the one with the worn leather arms, and opened her book. The pages were not crisp. They were frayed at the edges, soft from a hundred hands before hers, and the print was the colour of strong tea.

She read one page. Then another. Outside, the street grew quiet. Inside, the only sounds were the turning of pages and the slow ticking of the clock above the door.

Somewhere between the second chapter and the third, her phone buzzed in her pocket. She did not answer it. For the first time in months, the world could wait.

Chapter 3

When she finally looked up, the lamps had come on, one by one, down the long reading room. The librarian was shelving books in the far aisle, humming a tune she almost recognised.

"How long have I been here?" she asked.

"Long enough," he said. "That is how you know a book is good. You forget to count the hours."

Amara closed her book gently, the way you would close a door on a sleeping house, and carried it to the desk.

"May I take it home?"

"You may," he said. "But bring it back with a story of your own inside it. That is the price of this library."

She promised she would. And she did.

The End
